BLUE CROSS BLUE SHIELD - PRIOR AUTHORIZATION CLINICAL CRITERIA
Outpatient Lumbar Spine Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI)
CPT Codes: 72148, 72149, 72158

MEDICAL NECESSITY CRITERIA

Coverage for outpatient lumbar spine MRI is provided for members who meet the medical necessity criteria below. Prior authorization is required.

ALL requests for MRIs MUST be ordered by a M.D. or D.O. assuming responsibility for management of the patient.

Low Back pain complicated by any one of the following:

1. Persistent low back pain and/or radicular pain:
   - Beyond 6 weeks in duration; AND
   - In the last 6 months, symptoms have failed to improve despite 6 weeks of documented evidence-based conservative treatment that has included ALL of the following:
     a) Active participation in a physical therapy program OR active participation in Chiropractic directed care, either of which must include at least 3 visits and/or a discharge summary; AND
     b) Use of prescription or over-the-counter analgesics / NSAIDs (unless contraindicated); AND
     c) Activity modification / home exercise program.

2. Objective neurological deficits as indicated by ANY of the following:
   - Urinary urgency, frequency, retention, or overflow incontinence
   - Fecal incontinence
   - Spasticity
   - Sensory loss that is severe, persistent, or progressive
   - Objective muscle weakness of ≤ 3/5
   - Asymmetric hypo or hyperreflexia
   - Clonus
   - Pathologic reflexes (e.g., Babinski, Chaddock, Oppenheim, grasp)
   - Gait abnormality (e.g., ataxic)
   - Cauda equina syndrome
   - Saddle anesthesia

3. Documentation of a concern for/suspected malignancy with localized lumbar pain and ANY of the following:
   - Personal history or concurrent diagnosis of cancer, especially breast, lung, prostate, lymphoma, sarcoma, or kidney
   - Unexplained weight loss
   - Positive bone scan or suspicious lesion found on other imaging

4. Red flag conditions:
   - Major trauma
   - Minor trauma in a potentially osteoporotic patient
   - History of cancer
   - Fever / chills
   - Recent bacterial infection
   - IV drug abuse
   - Immune suppression
   - Pain that worsens when supine or at night

For non-red flag conditions, MRI may be appropriate after 1 month of symptoms and failure of conservative treatment.

A lumbar MRI used to evaluate uncomplicated degenerative disc disease or herniated nucleus pulposus is not considered medically necessary when a surgical intervention or other aggressive treatment is not under consideration.
